# Code of Conduct

Oeros brings together people from the robotics, embedded Linux and open source communities. We want it to be a welcoming, respectful and harassment-free place for everyone, whatever their experience, background or identity.

## Our codes of conduct

Oeros sits where three communities meet, and we follow the codes of conduct of all three:

- **[Linux Foundation Code of Conduct](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/about/code-of-conduct/).** The Yocto Project is a Linux Foundation project, and this code applies across its communities and events.
- **[Yocto Project Code of Conduct](https://www.yoctoproject.org/code-of-conduct/).** Oeros is built with the Yocto Project and OpenEmbedded, and changes flow upstream to those projects.
- **[OSRF Community Code of Conduct](https://osrf.github.io/code_of_conduct/).** Oeros ships ROS 2 and works closely with the ROS community, meta-ros and the ROS OpenEmbedded Community Working Group.

By taking part in any Oeros space, you agree to follow these codes. That includes repositories, issues, pull requests, Discussions, meetings, chat and events. The three codes share the same core expectations. Where they differ, the stricter expectation applies.

When you take part in an upstream space, such as ros/meta-ros, ROS Discourse, Zulip or Yocto Project mailing lists and meetings, that community's own code of conduct governs.

## In short

- Be respectful, patient and welcoming, especially to newcomers and people who are asking questions.
- Critique ideas and code, not people.
- Harassment, discrimination, personal attacks and unwelcome sexual attention are not acceptable.
- Respect people's privacy, and don't share private information without permission.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness unacceptable behaviour, please report it privately to the Oeros maintainers at **rob@robertwoolley.com**. All reports are handled confidentially, and we will not retaliate against anyone who reports in good faith.

If the behaviour happened in an upstream space, or involves someone acting for an upstream project, you can also report it there:

- Linux Foundation and Yocto Project: follow the reporting instructions in the [Linux Foundation](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/about/code-of-conduct/) and [Yocto Project](https://www.yoctoproject.org/code-of-conduct/) codes of conduct.
- ROS and OSRF: follow the reporting instructions in the [OSRF Community Code of Conduct](https://osrf.github.io/code_of_conduct/).

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, issues and other contributions that don't follow these codes. They may also warn, temporarily ban or permanently ban anyone whose behaviour is inappropriate, threatening, offensive or harmful.
